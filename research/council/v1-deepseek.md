# deepseek — v1
Score: 8/10
## Why not 10 (blocking items, most important first)
1. `demo/` — there is no demo video or any recording, and the submission's own definition of done says so (`IDEA.md:63`, `IDEA.md:72` "Left: … video"). The judges score the demo, and the one thing that sells Hurdle (Patient +1.9% vs par, Chaser fired on day 5.7, depositors never below par) is a JSON file plus a chart. A 10 has a 60–90 s capture recorded from the existing `?s=range-week#play` autoplay, with a three-line "what you are watching" caption (thick line = depositor, thin = NAV, grey = par, red = fired) on the page and in the README.
2. Live deployment / UI, chain 1301 — the deploy is real and matches the audited source (hook `0x23C2…90A0` has the right permission bits, both vaults have `carryBps=2000`, `minBondBps=200`, `maxTickDeviation=50`, `epochSeconds=3600`, 300 mUSDC bond, ~5,999 mUSDC NAV, one rebalance each, not fired; local runtime bytecode is identical except the immutables), but it is inert: at ~28 min after the deploy neither vault has settled, the pool has had no swap since the deploy (`oracleState` cardinality 2), `settle()` (`HurdleVault.sol:346`) is unrewarded so nothing will ever settle it, and `demo/ui` reads only `demo/out/*.json` — a judge cannot see the live system at all. A 10 runs a keeper (a shell loop or Foundry script doing a demo swap + `settle` hourly for ≥1 day, so the on-chain history contains the mechanism), adds a "Live" scenario/tab that reads the Sepolia vaults (`nav`, `bond`, `start`, `benchmark`), and puts the settle command in the README.
3. `HurdleVault.sol:394-399` + `:420-445` — post-fire collateral is stranded forever. After firing, `bond` tokens only leave through `_payShortfall` and `bondShares` only shrink on another miss; there is no sweep/claim, `postBond` is blocked (`:290`), and a parked full-range vault tracks par (the test allows 10 bp/epoch), so the remainder sits in the contract permanently. Range-week Chaser ends with 0.0091 of start capital unclaimed, and the pro-rata NAV behind any leftover `bondShares` is owned by nobody. A 10 burns `bondShares` and sweeps the residual `bond` to the then-current depositors pro rata at the firing settle (or in a permissionless post-fire call), or returns the remainder to the curator after a delay, and documents it.
4. `HurdleVault.sol:470-480` + `:48` and README:108-111 — the anti-wash fee cap is the remaining economic hole, and it scales with time: 300% APR × elapsed is 0.57 bp of NAV per 10 min but 5.75% for a week-long epoch, so an attacker who is also the pool's only other LP drains a 2% bond in ~2.4 days of washing, and because `settle()` is unrewarded (see item 2) nobody is paid to keep epochs short. The exact number is available: credit the fee term as what a real full-range LP of size `L_b` would earn, `L_b·fees/(L_active+L_b)` (snapshot `poolManager.getLiquidity()`), instead of a flat APR cap, and pay the settler a small bounty (from the carry) so the elapsed multiplier stays small. A 10 does at least the settler bounty plus a cap tied to the pool's realized fee APR.
5. `HurdleVault.sol:134` (and `:59`) — the vault trusts whatever hook the pool carries as its oracle, and there is no factory/registry. A malicious hook's `twapTick` sets NAV and par, so it sets the first-deposit share count, carry, the fire rule and the shortfall; the README's "depositors must check `poolKey.hooks`" is not enforcement, and a curator deploying on a hostile pool can over-mint the first deposit and then steal from later in-kind depositors. A 10 takes the canonical hook address in the constructor (`require(_key.hooks == canonical)`) or deploys through a small factory that pins it — a one-line check for a real security property.
6. `HurdleVault.sol:81` — shares are a bare `mapping(address => uint256)` with a `balanceOf`/`totalSupply` façade: no `transfer`/`approve`, no ERC20, no `previewDeposit`/`previewWithdraw`. That kills composability (no secondary market, no integrator, no listing) and makes the in-kind deposit unusable from a wallet: the amounts are a function of idle balances plus `ceil(L·s/t)` of the position at spot, with no on-chain quote, and the UI has no deposit path at all. A 10 ships ERC20 shares (counting curator/bond shares as counters so `_payShortfall` survives transfers), `previewDeposit`/`previewWithdraw`, and a deposit panel — or documents the non-transferability as a deliberate scope limit.
7. `test/` — scenario coverage is excellent, stateful coverage is missing. I reran all 94 tests and the replay (33,215 rows, 166 settles, Chaser fired day 5.65, timestamp/price/par CSV byte-identical to `demo/out/range-week.csv`, `par_check.py --twap` 0.000 bp over 167 settlements), but no Foundry invariant/handler suite exists for the code that holds the money. A 10 adds random sequences of deposit/withdraw/settle/rebalance/swap/postBond/price-jump asserting: depositor value ≥ par whenever `bondValue` covers the gap, Σclaims ≤ assets, share price does not jump on deposit/withdraw, and `deficit` per share is non-increasing.
8. `HurdleVault.sol:538-542`, README:112-115 — the TWAP-lag guard freezes the curator exactly when active management is most valuable: 24 re-centers refused by `PriceDeviation` in the range week, 19 in the −14% week, where Chaser is pinned in its last range (36 more refused `BondBelowFloor`). It is a documented trade-off and I accept the F2 reasoning, but a 10 allows a rebalance when a short TWAP and a longer TWAP agree within `maxTickDeviation` (still bounding the swap by both bands, plus the block-open reference), so the curator can act minutes after a real move without reopening the cross-block hold.
## Strengths (max 5 lines)
- Genuinely new mechanism — par as a closed-form full-range benchmark from the pool's own `feeGrowthGlobal`/price path — and it is verified, not asserted: 1.4e-18 of capital against real v4 accounting, 0.000 bp against an independent offchain reference over 167 hourly settlements, reproducibly.
- Rare honesty: real mainnet swaps, a week-selection rule fixed in advance, a byte-reproducible demo CSV, two independent audits with PoCs and mutation checks, and a README "Limits" section that states most of what I would otherwise have had to find.
- The money paths are carefully engineered (in-kind deposits, pro-rata deficit on exit, block-open fee growth, reference-tick price bands, self-call isolation so a failed settle/park cannot block exits) and each has a named test.
- Live on Unichain Sepolia with correct hook permission bits and the audited bytecode running (only immutables differ from the local build), two curator ranges deployed.
- Documentation and research footprint are far above hackathon norm; the kill table and prior-art sweep show the idea was chosen deliberately.
## Nits (optional, do not affect score)
- README:98-99 and `HurdleHook.sol:27-30` say the ring "spans at most 32 minutes": entries are only written ≥60 s apart and only when someone swaps, so the ring spans at least ~31 min and much longer in sparse trading. Nothing depends on it, but the sentence is backwards.
- `IDEA.md:62-63` and `:72` are stale: hook gas is now ~40k steady / ~60k cold (`research/phase3-twap.md:16-19`), not "12k steady / 34k", and the redeploy is done rather than pending. A judge comparing IDEA.md with the code sees an unfinished pitch.
- `HurdleVault.sol:504`: "valuing a concentrated position at TWAP … undervalues it: errs against the curator, never depositors" is not true in general — a token0-heavy position with spot below TWAP is over-valued at TWAP. Either state the direction per position composition or drop the claim.
- `HurdleVault.sol:305` says post-fire rebalances "cannot add risk"; they are uncounted and each still swaps at the band edge, so if the fire-time park failed an attacker can alternate spot inside the band to bleed a few bp per call. Small, but the comment overstates.
- The replay is opt-in (`Replay.t.sol:68`), so the headline claim can silently rot; a GitHub action running the two weeks plus `par_check.py` would cost 2 minutes and protect it.
- `demo/ui` is a good chart, but the default final frame shows both vaults "✓ ≥ par" while one is FIRED; surfacing the fire reason next to the badge would remove the ambiguity in a 30-second glance.
