## Why not 10 (blocking items, most important first)
1. `src/HurdleVault.sol:578-592`, `README.md:192-201` — The 80% APR cap bounds but does not eliminate the wash-fee benchmark attack. A dominant LP can recycle most fees paid while wash-swapping across blocks; the virtual full-range benchmark credits that fee growth without the vault earning it. An idle vault can therefore lose bond value at up to ~0.91 bp of NAV per hour, and the documented estimate drains a 10% bond in about 46 days. Make benchmark fee credit depend on fees actually received by the vault, or remove this input; otherwise add a hard cumulative exposure bound and a mitigation that protects the bond over long-lived vaults.
2. `src/HurdleVault.sol:528-561` — The revised covered-settle condition weakens the depositor guarantee compared with treating “covered” as bond >= depositors’ share of a shortfall. `_payShortfall` computes the depositor shortfall slice (`owed`) but tokens entering the vault are shared across all remaining shares, including curator free shares; if the bond only equals `owed`, depositors receive less than `owed` by the curator’s share fraction (`x/t`). Example: if curator/bond shares are 20% of supply, a bond that exactly equals depositors’ 80% slice leaves depositors up to 20% of that slice unpaid until a later settle. Either require/define coverage as the whole shortfall as the README now does, and make every handler/test use that condition, or change the payout math so the weaker bond threshold still makes depositors whole in the same settlement. Add a regression at nonzero curator free-share balance.
3. `src/HurdleVault.sol` — The custom share, deficit, bond, and carry accounting remains high impact and has no external audit of the final source. The audit3 fixes address the identified dilution mismatch and fired-unparked deposit path, but regressions in gross-up or share rounding could still move value between depositors and curator. Obtain an independent review of this exact version and turn any findings into regression tests before treating it as production-ready.
